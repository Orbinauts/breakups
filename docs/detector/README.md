# The detector, as built

What `src/` does, module by module, with the gates it decides by, the states an alert passes through, what each phase of the build bought in the scorecard's numbers, and where the parent-step channel's ceiling lies and on what evidence. The fitting notes (`d1-fitting.md` to `d5-fitting.md`) record each phase's sweeps on the training split; `kpi/latest.md` carries the scorecard as last run; `events.md` dissects every reported event. Every threshold is in `src/params.ts`, tagged physical, policy or fitted with the sweep that chose it (`PARAMETER_PROVENANCE`).

## The signal and the clock

A breakup leaves two traces in public data. The parent's own orbit jumps, by metres to kilometres, in the element sets the catalogue publishes for it; and days to weeks later new objects appear under the parent's launch. The owner's definition scores the first trace: a detection is an alert on the parent's step raised before the first fragment's element set is published, since afterwards the tool only repackages what the catalogue said. The fragments confirm; they never detect. So the detector decides on the few sets of the first days after the event, and everything below is built to make that decision as sharp as the parent's own data allow, and then to say, from the pieces, whether it was right.

## The modules

**`elements.ts`**: the light row the detector reads, one set of one object at one epoch: the six mean elements, the drag terms, the element set number, the publication time where the catalogue gives it, and the catalogue's class of the body; the semi-major axis, the apsides and the regime (low, medium, highly elliptical or geosynchronous orbit) derived from it with the engine's constants.

**`series.ts`**: what the detector keeps of one object between runs. A ring of the last thirty-two sets sorted by epoch, each with the one-step-ahead residual it left on arrival; re-published epochs replace their earlier values and older epochs slot in without a residual, since the catalogue re-issues a third of its sets in some years. A set that leaves the orbit by more than fifty kilometres of semi-major axis, half a degree of inclination or five degrees of node off its J2 precession is held in quarantine until the next set says whether the orbit moved there, since a cross-tagged set does that and no breakup does. The object's own scatter is a robust short scatter of the ring's residuals floored by a long memory with a half-year half-life, so a calm week cannot make an object look quieter than it is; before it has eight residuals a regime prior scaled by the drag term and the day's flux and Ap stands in, which is how a day-old stage is caught. The two-sided CUSUM, the object's accepted steps over two years and the day bins of its tracking cadence live here too.

**`model.ts`**: the trailing fit the next set is predicted from: a Huber-weighted local polynomial of the semi-major axis, the perigee radius and the inclination over the window (sixteen sets in low and medium orbits, the whole ring in highly elliptical ones, where the mean elements wander with the Moon and the Sun), quadratic in time where six sets over three days allow it, linear with fewer, the single set's own drag rate with fewer than three. The prediction's sigma is the object's own scatter times the extrapolation's leverage, plus a drag term that grows with the drag term and the gap, so a high-drag object's next set is allowed to miss by what drag can do.

**`environment.ts`**: each run's residuals read by band (fifty kilometres of perigee height in low orbit, one band per other regime) for the day's common-mode drag (the median residual per unit of drag term and gap, which a storm moves for every object at once) and the live scale (the robust scatter of the normalised residuals, floored at one), so a storm raises the bar for everyone and an intact low-drag body hundreds of times above its own scatter still stands out. The space-weather indices scale the prior, enter the drag prediction and set a storm hold at Kp 6; the catalogue-wide normaliser still runs when they are missing.

**`step.ts`**: the step estimated over the post sets against the frozen pre-step fit: a two-segment model with a free settling slope (the catalogue's fit converges on a new orbit over several sets), the step's size in units of its own sigma at the first post set and at the post sets' centroid, the median ratio of the later sets' residuals to the first jump (a bad set is undone by the next, a real step is kept), and the evidence for a step against a drift drag could produce (the drift model's weighted squares less the step model's, each slope held by a prior of what noise and drag do in a day). The same on the perigee and the inclination, and the CUSUM as a side channel for a slow move no single set shows.

**`impulse.ts`**: what a measured step implies by Gauss's variational equations: a tangential component from the step in the semi-major axis at the point on the orbit the apsides place it, a normal component from the inclination step where it is at least a hundredth of a degree, and the least radial component the eccentricity change needs beyond a single tangential impulse's reach, with that excess as a chi-squared.

**`manoeuvre.ts`**: the object's own record. Every persistent step joins its accepted steps over two years, whatever verdict it got, and a new step is classed by them alone: station-keeping (four or more steps in the year, regular or alternating), campaign (four in five with the step's sign, or a young payload's first raise), disposal (the perigee dropped twenty kilometres with the apogee kept, or a raise of fifty kilometres in low orbit or two hundred at geosynchronous altitude), occasional (a past step on no pattern) or unique (no step in two years). The tests by which a step on a manoeuvring record is still raised: ten times the record's median, opposite to every one of at least two past steps, or an eccentricity change one tangential burn cannot explain (chi-squared over a hundred). The catalogue's class of the object is read in one place only, a young payload's first raise, never as a gate.

**`fragments.ts`**: the per-launch channel. Every launch keeps the bodies seen under it with their first set's orbit, when each was first seen, its new bodies by month, and its cloud memory; the catalogue keeps the high-water mark of designated numbers against time. On a run that brings a launch new bodies: the burst over the launch's own background (a Poisson tail of the fortnight's count above the mark against the rate read off the year before, floored at half a body); the numbering (a body at or under the mark at the event, or with a set before it, is a returning old body); the plane of each piece against each candidate parent, the node adjusted for each orbit's own J2 precession from the event to the piece's first set, as a distance in units of the tolerances (0.2 degrees of inclination, 1.5 of node, within three); and the cloud's velocity and Gabbard spread, with a rideshare's deployments and a young launch's own objects excluded. Analyst objects go to a pseudo-launch per range and confirm nothing, since their numbers are reused.

**`event-time.ts`**: the event's instant from the two sets either side of the step, both propagated with the engine on a minute grid from six hours before the pre set's epoch to the post set's; the along-track separation fitted as a line whose root is the event and whose scatter over the drift is its sigma, with the per-revolution closest approaches listed so the revolution ambiguity is stated rather than hidden.

**`fusion.ts`**: what an alert is and keeps. A persistent step opens an alert, suspected or manoeuvre, that stays thirty days for the pieces or a notice; while open it keeps reading the object's sets against the frozen pre-step model and records whether the step still held after three and five post sets and after one, three, five and seven days, the tracking cadence after the step, the pieces it saw, and the confirmation rules' reading. Its row in the log is what the scorecard's tier table and the confirmation sweep read.

**`run.ts`**: one run in three passes. Measure: each set screened against its ring, predicted from the object's trailing model (or, for a post set of a live candidate, from the candidate's frozen pre-step model), pushed with its residual, and the body noted under its launch. Environment: the run's residuals by band, and the weather. Decide: a set past six sigma opens a candidate on the bracket from the last set before it to itself (a watch verdict shows it on a quiet object), each later set re-estimates the step, and the candidate closes as a persistent step, a bad set, a lapse or a fade. A persistent step is fused with the record (`suspected` where the record does not explain it, `manoeuvre` otherwise), timed, and opens an alert. Then the launch pass: every launch the run brought bodies to is judged, open alerts on its parents are confirmed by pieces under the confirmation rules, notices are applied, a burst with pieces and no alert is listed as `cloud-only`, and alerts past the time to live close.

**`params.ts`**, **`log.ts`**, **`detector.ts`**: every threshold with its provenance; one log row per candidate, alert and launch judgement with every signal, so the sweeps re-threshold offline; the shapes the scorecard scores.

## The gates

- Candidate: a set that leaves the object's model by six sigma (the object's own scatter, the extrapolation's leverage, the drag term, the band's common mode and live scale).
- Watch: the first post set on a quiet object (its scatter measured, nothing closed on it in a month, no step raised in a year, the jump alone past the persistence size), shown and counted by nothing.
- Persistent: the step holds at ten sigma over two post sets spanning eighteen hours in low and medium orbits (six above, where sets are sparse and drag cannot mimic a step) or three post sets whatever they span, three under a storm hold or run stress; the later sets keep at least half the first jump; the evidence against a drift is at least ten. A candidate whose later sets keep under half the jump is a bad set; one with no post set in five days (longer above low orbit) lapses and its set is dropped so the step is measured again; one not persistent after eight post sets fades.
- Raised (`suspected`, the detection): a persistent step whose record does not explain it: unique, or atypical on a manoeuvring record by size, direction or geometry. A step older than the time to live when it is raised (a bundle's batch of decades-old sets) opens no alert.
- Confirmed: pieces under the launch numbered above the mark at the step, first published after it, in the parent's plane, under a launch older than a month and quiet in the year before (at most two new bodies a fortnight), under the fifth phase's rules: a cloud velocity of at least a metre a second; not on a debris-classed parent first catalogued under ninety days before its step; where the launch remembers a cloud within a year, only pieces out of that cloud's plane and only where the parent is not in it; on a debris-classed parent under an active cloud, only pieces nearer its plane than the cloud's nearest body; a single piece only on a parent that is not debris under a launch that gained nothing in the year before and nothing else in the window. A public notice naming the object confirms it too.
- Expired: nothing in thirty days. Dismissed: bodies came under the launch and every one was old. Cloud-only: a burst with consistent pieces at a metre a second or more and no alert on any parent, listed once a month per launch for review.

## The states

`candidate` (judged, unlogged until it closes) → `watch` (shown) → `suspected` (the detection) or `manoeuvre` (terminal, counted as nothing) → `confirmed` by pieces or a notice, or `dismissed`, or `expired`. A `manoeuvre` the pieces confirm is promoted and says so. `cloud-only` is the catalogue's own report read back and is never a detection.

## What each phase bought

The headline line is breakups reported to the day or better on the validation split, the odd years; the training split is the even years, where every fitted value was chosen.

| Phase | Built | Validation headline | False alarms | What it bought |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 `step-v1` | the parent-step channel on every object | 12 detected, 2 late, 18 missed of 32 (38%) | 74,489 | every mover caught, day-old stages and sparse regimes included; 96% of the false alarms were payload manoeuvres |
| D2 `step-v2` | the object's record and the impulse | 11, 2, 19 of 32 (34%) | 15,623 | 58,049 manoeuvre verdicts silenced; the gate cost Cosmos 2499's second event, whose one past step was its first; the five-day margin in deep space gained the ullage motors on the training split |
| D3 `fusion-v3` | the fragments and the event's time | 11, 2, 19 of 32 (34%) | 15,645 | the Confirmed line (5 right, 14 wrong on validation), the review list, timing to a median of 35 minutes from 18 hours, the tier table: a week of waiting halves the false alarms and loses a third of the detections |
| D5 `fusion-v4` | the confirmation rules, the Confirmed block, the dissection, the freeze | 11, 2, 19 of 32 (34%) | 15,546 | the Confirmed line the product can show: on validation 14 confirmed alerts from 43, precision 36% on the step and 57% on the object from 12% and 26%; 5 of the 36 targets the catalogue reported confirmed, a median of 4 days after the alert; `d5-fitting.md` lists every confirmation before and after, `events.md` every event with its cause |

The fourth phase of the design (`event-time`, GEO/HEO tuning) was folded into the third: the event's time is there, and the highly elliptical line was dissected in `d3-fitting.md` and found to be a limit of the data rather than of the thresholds. The first three phases' rows are as their notes reported them; the fifth phase corrected the scorecard's catalogue clock (the mark and a fragment's first set by the catalogue's publication order, not by back-dated epochs; `d5-fitting.md`), under which the third phase's validation line reads the same and its false alarms 15,561, and its leads over the catalogue were overstated.

## The ceiling, and the evidence

The parent-step channel cannot reach the owner's targets (nine in ten detected, under one false alarm in ten) from the parent's data alone, and the evidence is in the numbers rather than in an argument:

- Half the headline misses are parents that did not measurably move (`events.md`, the totals by cause: the parent had sets between the event and the catalogue and none left its model by six sigma). Low-velocity events, anomalous events and the ullage motors whose perigee rides at two hundred kilometres with the drag term wandering leave no step a set stands out from. Only the fragments can see these, and the owner's definition scores them after the fragments.
- A quarter are the clock: the parent had no set between the event and the catalogue's first fragment, or one set, under the owner's rule that one set cannot raise an alert. The deep-space events' sets come days to weeks apart.
- The rest are the record gate (a breakup step the parent's own record of manoeuvres explains, as Cosmos 2421's twenty-one lowerings explained its breakup step), ramping fits (the catalogue converging on the new orbit over several sets, under-reading the first post set), and the margin (the catalogue's step days from the stated time in deep space, where the stated time is itself inferred).
- The false alarms that remain hold for a week as a breakup's step does: lone manoeuvres on satellites that seldom move, a young satellite's second step the other way from its first, and burns whose eccentricity change one tangential impulse cannot explain. The tier table shows waiting does not separate them (`kpi/latest.md`): precision stays under a percent at every tier. The two parent-side signals the owner asked about, the drag term's ratio across the step and the tracking cadence after it, sit inside the false alarms' distribution (`d3-fitting.md`).

What separates a breakup from a manoeuvre at the first sets is the pieces, which by the owner's definition confirm rather than detect. So the product has two lines: the parent-step alert, which the scorecard shows catches about a third to a half of the headline events before the catalogue at a precision the data do not allow to rise; and the Confirmed line, which the fifth phase makes precise enough to show, days after the step. The decision this leaves the owner is which of the two the product leads with.
