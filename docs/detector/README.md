# The detector, as built

What `src/` does, module by module, with the gates it decides by, the states an alert passes through, what each phase of the build bought in the scorecard's numbers, and where the parent-step channel's ceiling lies and on what evidence. The fitting notes (`d1-fitting.md` to `d6-fitting.md`, `d7-scope.md` for the scope study, `d8-audit.md` for the truth audit, `d9-confirmed.md` for the Confirmed channel, `d10-confirmed.md` for the methodology fixes, the twins and the common-origin test, and `d11-confirmed.md` for the crewed and cargo craft, the truth's tight-origin candidates and the reentry and small-batch rules) record each phase's sweeps on the training split; `kpi/latest.md` carries the scorecard as last run; `events.md` dissects every reported event. Every threshold is in `src/params.ts`, tagged physical, policy or fitted with the sweep that chose it (`PARAMETER_PROVENANCE`).

## The signal and the clock

A breakup leaves two traces in public data. The parent's own orbit jumps, by metres to kilometres, in the element sets the catalogue publishes for it; and days to weeks later new objects appear under the parent's launch. The owner's first definition scored the first trace: a detection was an alert on the parent's step raised before the first fragment's element set is published. Eight phases showed that the parent's orbit cannot carry nine detections in ten at under one false alarm in ten: a third of the reported breakups never move their parent measurably, and the steps that remain after every discriminator look like the breakups' (the ceiling, below). On 2026-10-10 the owner moved the target to the second trace (`docs/decisions/0007-the-target-is-on-confirmed-alerts.md`): a Confirmed alert is a breakup the detector attributes to a parent from fresh fragments in the parent's plane, with or without a step seen first on it, and its lead is measured against the first agency statement rather than the catalogue. So the detector now reads both traces for what each gives: the parent's step, early and imprecise, as the secondary lines; and the pieces, which name the parent, confirm its step where one was seen and time the event by their convergence on it, as the target.

## The modules

**`elements.ts`**: the light row the detector reads, one set of one object at one epoch: the six mean elements, the drag terms, the element set number, the publication time where the catalogue gives it, and the catalogue's class of the body; the semi-major axis, the apsides and the regime (low, medium, highly elliptical or geosynchronous orbit) derived from it with the engine's constants.

**`series.ts`**: what the detector keeps of one object between runs. A ring of the last thirty-two sets sorted by epoch, each with the one-step-ahead residual it left on arrival; re-published epochs replace their earlier values and older epochs slot in without a residual, since the catalogue re-issues a third of its sets in some years. A set that leaves the orbit by more than fifty kilometres of semi-major axis, half a degree of inclination or five degrees of node off its J2 precession is held in quarantine until the next set says whether the orbit moved there, since a cross-tagged set does that and no breakup does. The object's own scatter is a robust short scatter of the ring's residuals floored by a long memory with a half-year half-life, so a calm week cannot make an object look quieter than it is; before it has eight residuals a regime prior scaled by the drag term and the day's flux and Ap stands in, which is how a day-old stage is caught. The two-sided CUSUM, the object's accepted steps over two years and the day bins of its tracking cadence live here too.

**`model.ts`**: the trailing fit the next set is predicted from: a Huber-weighted local polynomial of the semi-major axis, the perigee radius and the inclination over the window (sixteen sets in low and medium orbits, the whole ring in highly elliptical ones, where the mean elements wander with the Moon and the Sun), quadratic in time where six sets over three days allow it, linear with fewer, the single set's own drag rate with fewer than three. The prediction's sigma is the object's own scatter times the extrapolation's leverage, plus a drag term that grows with the drag term and the gap, so a high-drag object's next set is allowed to miss by what drag can do.

**`environment.ts`**: each run's residuals read by band (fifty kilometres of perigee height in low orbit, one band per other regime) for the day's common-mode drag (the median residual per unit of drag term and gap, which a storm moves for every object at once) and the live scale (the robust scatter of the normalised residuals, floored at one), so a storm raises the bar for everyone and an intact low-drag body hundreds of times above its own scatter still stands out. The space-weather indices scale the prior, enter the drag prediction and set a storm hold at Kp 6; the catalogue-wide normaliser still runs when they are missing.

**`step.ts`**: the step estimated over the post sets against the frozen pre-step fit: a two-segment model with a free settling slope (the catalogue's fit converges on a new orbit over several sets), the step's size in units of its own sigma at the first post set and at the post sets' centroid, the median ratio of the later sets' residuals to the first jump (a bad set is undone by the next, a real step is kept), and the evidence for a step against a drift drag could produce (the drift model's weighted squares less the step model's, each slope held by a prior of what noise and drag do in a day). The same on the perigee and the inclination, and the CUSUM as a side channel for a slow move no single set shows.

**`impulse.ts`**: what a measured step implies by Gauss's variational equations: a tangential component from the step in the semi-major axis at the point on the orbit the apsides place it, a normal component from the inclination step where it is at least a hundredth of a degree, and the least radial component the eccentricity change needs beyond a single tangential impulse's reach, with that excess as a chi-squared.

**`manoeuvre.ts`**: the object's own record. Every persistent step joins its accepted steps over two years, whatever verdict it got, and a new step is classed by them alone: station-keeping (four or more steps in the year, regular or alternating), campaign (four in five with the step's sign, or a young payload's first raise), disposal (the perigee dropped twenty kilometres with the apogee kept, or a raise of fifty kilometres in low orbit or two hundred at geosynchronous altitude), occasional (a past step on no pattern) or unique (no step in two years). The tests by which a step on a manoeuvring record is still raised: ten times the record's median, opposite to every one of at least two past steps, or an eccentricity change one tangential burn cannot explain (chi-squared over a hundred). The catalogue's class of the object is read here in one place only, a young payload's first raise; the sixth phase reads it in a second, to say whether the explanations below apply at all.

**`maintenance.ts`**: where the object has flown. Every object keeps the lowest and highest semi-major axis it showed in each of the last twelve calendar months, frozen on a candidate when it opens so a jump is never judged against itself; a persistent step whose post-step level lands inside a month's range, or within a tolerance of it (a few times the object's own scatter, or a floor in kilometres, both fitted), is a return to a held altitude, which is what a drag make-up raise or a seasonal lowering looks like and what a breakup kick, which lands anywhere, rarely does.

**`siblings.ts`**: what the object's company did. The catalogue-wide ledger keeps every persistent step of the last four weeks with the orbit it was made on; a new step counts the other objects of its launch, and of other launches in its shell (the same inclination within a tenth of a degree, the semi-major axis within fifty kilometres), that made a step of the same sign and a size within a factor of two in the fortnight before: enough of them and the step is a coordinated campaign, since a breakup is one object alone. And for an object under the fitted age, the median altitude of its launch's other payloads as their states hold it: a step that carries the object at least half its length towards that altitude is a deployment climb.

The operational status (`elements.ts`, `opsStatus`) is the satellite catalogue's `OPS_STATUS_CODE` as the caller hands it, folded to working (`+`, `P`, `B`, `S`, `X`), nonoperational (`-`), decayed (`D`, read as nothing) or unknown. Since the seventh phase it is read as of the step (`operationalAt`): where the caller gives the window the payload fell silent in (GCAT's end of transmission, `endedByMs` and `endedFromMs` on the element row), a step more than thirty days before it is a commanded satellite's, one more than thirty days after it a dead one's, and one within the month either side reads nothing, since the end of a satellite that broke up is often the breakup itself; with no date, only a working status today is read. The sixth decision record says why, and `d7-scope.md` gives the scorecard with the dated status, today's and none.

**`scope.ts`**: the scoped line. A predicate on the parent at the time of the step (its class, its status as of the step, its regime, perigee and drag term, its age from launch, which the element row now carries as `launchedMs`, and the days since its last persistent step), and gates on a raised step beyond the whole-catalogue line's (its size in sigma and in metres, its along-track impulse, the kick one tangential impulse cannot explain, the drag term's change, whether a manoeuvre explanation applied), with the persistence tier it must hold to. Every persistent step's row and verdict carry whether its parent was in the scope (`scopeIn`) and whether the step also passed the gates (`scoped`); an open alert that is both raises one `scoped` verdict when its step reaches the tier still holding. The scorecard scores the scoped line on the reported events whose parent was in the scope at the event and the alerts it raised, beside the whole catalogue's lines, with the headline events it leaves out counted. The values are `DETECTOR_PARAMETERS.scope`, chosen in `d7-scope.md`.

**`fragments.ts`**: the per-launch channel. Every launch keeps the bodies seen under it with their first set's orbit, when each was first seen, its new bodies by month, and its cloud memory; the catalogue keeps the high-water mark of designated numbers against time. On a run that brings a launch new bodies: the burst over the launch's own background (a Poisson tail of the fortnight's count above the mark against the rate read off the year before, floored at half a body); the numbering (a body at or under the mark at the event, or with a set before it, is a returning old body); the plane of each piece against each candidate parent, the node adjusted for each orbit's own J2 precession from the event to the piece's first set, as a distance in units of the tolerances (0.2 degrees of inclination, 1.5 of node, within three); and the cloud's velocity and Gabbard spread, with a rideshare's deployments and a young launch's own objects excluded. Each piece carries every candidate parent whose plane it lies within the bound of, so the attribution sees where the plane test alone cannot name the parent. The mark is the designated numbers' below the analyst ranges: until the ninth phase a body designated under a launch but numbered in them lifted it in 2020 over every piece catalogued after, which the ninth phase found and fixed. Analyst objects go to a pseudo-launch per range; their numbers are reused, so they confirm an open alert only where the parameters let them and the plane test names that alert's parent alone (off as fitted).

**`event-time.ts`**: the event's instant from the two sets either side of the step, both propagated with the engine on a minute grid from six hours before the pre set's epoch to the post set's; the along-track separation fitted as a line whose root is the event and whose scatter over the drift is its sigma, with the per-revolution closest approaches listed so the revolution ambiguity is stated rather than hidden. And, where no step was seen, the event's instant from the pieces (`eventFromPieces`): every piece was where the parent was at the breakup, so the pieces' first sets and the parent's last set before them, propagated back, converge there; the angle of each piece from the parent about the parent's orbit normal passes through zero once a lap, the crossing the pieces share is found on a coarse grid of up to 180 days and refined on the minute grid by the least root-mean-square distance of the pieces from the parent (the miss), and the sigma is the miss over the pieces' relative speed or their crossings' scatter, whichever is larger. A single piece's laps cannot be told apart, and the count of crossings within twice the least miss says so. Since the tenth phase each piece's own crossing nearest the instant the pieces agree on is kept too, with its lap about the parent, and read as the common origin (`originOf`): the median crossing, the half-widths within which half and three quarters of the pieces crossed, in hours and in laps, and the pieces' age at it. The median crossing is the event's instant where its sigma is the finer; pieces made within an hour of one instant (three at least) override the rules that read a launch's history as an old cloud (`overridesHistory`); pieces whose instant is at or before the launch's first sighting are the launch's own objects. As a gate the test is off: on the training years it costs half the confirmations at every bound (`d10-confirmed.md`). Where the first sets are days after the event the pieces converge to a few kilometres and the instant to minutes (Cosmos 2251, Nimiq 6's Briz-M, Iridium 91); where they are weeks after, SGP4 run backwards over a low cloud's drag misses by thousands of kilometres and the sigma, the pieces' scatter, says so.

**`fusion.ts`**: the decision, and what an alert is and keeps. `judgeStep` weighs a persistent step's evidence: the manoeuvre explanations (the object's own record; a return to a held altitude; enough siblings; a deployment climb; a working status, where the status is read, the last four on payloads only unless the parameter extends them) against the breakup signatures (the record's four tests, the geometry one only where the radial component reaches the fitted kick; a lowering on a record of nothing but raises, not disposal-shaped, from the fitted number of raises; and a nonoperational status on an object with no record of moving itself, where the parameters read it). The step is raised where no explanation applies or any signature does; otherwise it is a manoeuvre; the reasons say which applied, and the explanation and signature bits are logged so the sweep re-applies the whole decision. A persistent step then opens an alert, suspected or manoeuvre, that stays thirty days for the pieces or a notice; while open it keeps reading the object's sets against the frozen pre-step model and records whether the step still held after three and five post sets and after one, three, five and seven days, the tracking cadence after the step, the object's one-step-ahead residual scatter in the second to fourth week against its scatter before (the damage ratio: a tumbling object or a changed shape scatters more), the pieces it saw, and the confirmation rules' reading. Its row in the log is what the scorecard's tier table, the separation report and the confirmation sweep read.

**`run.ts`**: one run in three passes. Measure: each set screened against its ring, predicted from the object's trailing model (or, for a post set of a live candidate, from the candidate's frozen pre-step model), pushed with its residual, and the body noted under its launch. Environment: the run's residuals by band, and the weather. Decide: a set past six sigma opens a candidate on the bracket from the last set before it to itself (a watch verdict shows it on a quiet object), each later set re-estimates the step, and the candidate closes as a persistent step, a bad set, a lapse or a fade. A persistent step is read against the record, the envelope, the ledger, the launch-mates and the status, fused by `judgeStep` (`suspected` where no manoeuvre explains it or a breakup signature overrides, `manoeuvre` otherwise), timed, noted in the ledger, and opens an alert. Then the launch pass: every launch the run brought bodies to is judged, open alerts on its parents are confirmed by pieces under the confirmation rules, the pieces in the plane of a body with no open alert are attributed to it (`attributeCloud`, the Confirmed channel: the body named is, of those whose plane the pieces share, the one their semi-major axes centre on; it is told from the others by that centre, by the pieces' convergence on it, or by a persistent step it alone made; the confirmation rules and the attribution's own apply; the verdict is `confirmed` with no step, its event the pieces' convergence), a launch with few debris-classed bodies tries its older ones as parents for pieces in no other body's plane, notices are applied, a burst with pieces nobody was named for is listed as `cloud-only`, and alerts past the time to live close.

**`visiting.ts`**: the launches of the crewed spacecraft and the stations' cargo craft in orbit since 2005, generated from the satellite catalogue's names by `npm run archive:visiting` (`scripts/archive/visiting.ts`), the default of `visitingLaunches`: what such a craft leaves in orbit is released on purpose, so its pieces confirm nothing but on the launch's rocket body. The caller may also pass the catalogue's decay days (`DetectorInput.decays`), which the confirmation rules read from each day on: pieces first seen after their parent reentered are the reentry's.

**`params.ts`**, **`log.ts`**, **`detector.ts`**: every threshold with its provenance; one log row per candidate, alert and launch judgement with every signal, so the sweeps re-threshold offline; the shapes the scorecard scores.

## The gates

- Candidate: a set that leaves the object's model by six sigma (the object's own scatter, the extrapolation's leverage, the drag term, the band's common mode and live scale).
- Watch: the first post set on a quiet object (its scatter measured, nothing closed on it in a month, no step raised in a year, the jump alone past the persistence size), shown and counted by nothing.
- Persistent: the step holds at ten sigma over two post sets spanning eighteen hours in low and medium orbits (six above, where sets are sparse and drag cannot mimic a step) or three post sets whatever they span, three under a storm hold or run stress; the later sets keep at least half the first jump; the evidence against a drift is at least ten. A candidate whose later sets keep under half the jump is a bad set; one with no post set in five days (longer above low orbit) lapses and its set is dropped so the step is measured again; one not persistent after eight post sets fades.
- Raised (`suspected`, the detection): a persistent step no manoeuvre explains, or one a breakup signature overrides. The explanations: the object's own record (any class but unique); a return to an altitude the object held in the last year (within the fitted tolerance of a held range); at least the fitted number of siblings (launch-mates or shell-mates with a like step in the fortnight before); a deployment climb on an object under the fitted age; and a working status, where the status is read. The last four apply to payloads only. The signatures: ten times the record's median; opposite to every one of at least two past steps; an eccentricity change one tangential burn cannot explain, with a radial component at least the fitted kick; a lowering on a record of nothing but raises from the fitted number of raises, not disposal-shaped; and a nonoperational status on a unique step, where read. The fitted values are in `d6-fitting.md`. A step older than the time to live when it is raised (a bundle's batch of decades-old sets) opens no alert.
- Confirmed by attribution (the Confirmed channel, the target since the seventh decision record): pieces under the launch numbered above the mark, first published after the cloud's first set, in the plane of a body of the launch with no open alert, under the confirmation rules below and these: the launch older than a month (`attributionYoungLaunch` off), at least two pieces kept (`attributionMinPieces`), the run bringing new bodies to at most two launches older than a year (`attributionBatchLaunchesMax`: more is the catalogue listing a batch of old objects), and, where the pieces share the plane of other bodies of the launch, the body named told from them by the centre of the pieces' axes (three times nearer, floored at two kilometres), by their convergence (three times smaller a miss) or by a persistent step it alone made, in which last case the bodies it could not be told from are named beside it as the verdict's candidates (twins, `Verdict.candidates`; the launch's own debris of its first season counts where it left at most thirty); a burst nobody was named for in the half year before does not keep its own cloud from being attributed (`attributionUnnamedGraceDays`). Since the tenth phase: at least three pieces (`attributionMinPieces`); nothing under a crewed station's launch (`stationLaunches`); no pieces whose common instant is at or before the launch's first sighting (`originLaunchMarginDays`); and three pieces or more crossing the parent within an hour of one instant (not the remembered cloud's own) lift the remembered cloud, the backlog and the cataloguing batch (`originOverrideHours`). Since the eleventh phase: nothing under the launch of a crewed spacecraft or a station's cargo craft but on its rocket body (`visitingLaunches`, generated from the satellite catalogue's names); nothing from pieces first seen on or after their parent's reentry day, as the catalogue publishes it (`reentryRefuses`, the decay read only from its day on); and an attribution of three or four pieces needs three quarters of them crossing the parent within 48 hours of their median (`smallBatchOriginHours`). The fitted values are in `d9-confirmed.md`, `d10-confirmed.md` and `d11-confirmed.md`.
- Confirmed: pieces under the launch numbered above the mark at the step, first published after it, in the parent's plane, under a launch older than a month and quiet in the year before (at most two new bodies a fortnight), under the fifth phase's rules: a cloud velocity of at least a metre a second; not on a debris-classed parent first catalogued under ninety days before its step; where the launch remembers a cloud within a year, only pieces out of that cloud's plane and only where the parent is not in it; on a debris-classed parent under an active cloud, only pieces nearer its plane than the cloud's nearest body; a single piece only on a parent that is not debris under a launch that gained nothing in the year before and nothing else in the window. A public notice naming the object confirms it too.
- Scoped (the scoped line's alert): a suspected step on a parent launched five years or more before it, of twenty sigma or more, with an along-track impulse of at least three centimetres a second and an eccentricity change one tangential impulse cannot explain (chi-squared a hundred), a drag term changed at least two-fold where read, once it has held seven days.
- Expired: nothing in thirty days. Dismissed: bodies came under the launch and every one was old. Cloud-only: a burst with consistent pieces at a metre a second or more and no alert on any parent, listed once a month per launch for review.

## The states

`candidate` (judged, unlogged until it closes) → `watch` (shown) → `suspected` (the detection) or `manoeuvre` (terminal, counted as nothing) → `scoped` where the scoped line raises it → `confirmed` by pieces or a notice, or `dismissed`, or `expired`. A `manoeuvre` the pieces confirm is promoted and says so. A `confirmed` verdict with no step is the Confirmed channel's attribution: no alert was open on the parent, and the pieces named it. `cloud-only` is a burst nobody could be named for, listed for review.

## The Confirmed line, and its ceiling

The eleventh phase (`d11-confirmed.md`) exempted the crewed and cargo craft, refused pieces after their parent's reentry and small attributed batches with no tight origin: on the training years 22 of 35 at 8% false, on the held-out years 17 of 23 (74%) at 31%; what is left on both sides is a reported cloud's late pieces, twins named wrongly, single pieces and small batches confirming a step, and larger batches under old launches, half of the held-out ones near debris GCAT alone dates. The tenth phase (`d10-confirmed.md`) scored from 2006, exempted the stations, named twins at the launch and read the pieces' common origin: on the training years 22 of 35 at 22% false, on the held-out years 17 of 23 (74%) at 38%. The common origin separates true clouds from false ones by their medians and not by their distributions, so it overrides and times rather than gates; the false confirmations and misses left are single pieces, small batches under old launches, and stages told from their payloads or siblings wrongly. What the ninth phase found below stands, with those numbers.

The ninth phase moved the target to the pieces (`d9-confirmed.md`). With every rule off the attribution confirms 28 of the 35 headline breakups of the training years whose fragments were catalogued in the parent's plane, at 553 false confirmations; the rules that make it usable (the cataloguing batch, the fewest pieces, the fifth phase's rules against old clouds) leave 22 at 18, and on the held-out years 18 of 25 at 31, twelve of those in 2005, the detector's first year. The misses the rules cannot reach are bodies of one launch in one plane and one orbit, which nothing public tells apart (twin ullage motors, a fairing and its siblings, a stage and its payload), and single pieces; the false confirmations left are pieces of old launches catalogued one or two at a time and the station's jettisoned items, which look like a small cloud in every number the catalogue gives. Nine in ten at one in ten is not reached on either split, and the training sweeps show no rule that trades a confirmation for fewer than several false ones.

## The ceiling of the parent-step channel, and the evidence

The seventh phase tested the last open lever the owner named, the population: whether some orbits or objects are always missed and can be left out, and whether restricting to dead satellites, to low orbit, to old or quiet objects or to low drag, with every strict gate the log allows, reaches the target on what remains. It does not, on either split (`d7-scope.md`): a perigee under 400 kilometres is never caught and can be excluded for nothing, but every population keeps false alarms whose steps are as large, as kicked and as lasting as the breakups', and with three to five headline detections a split the target allows none. The scoped line is the nearest point, kept beside the whole catalogue's line.

The sixth phase tested the part of this section that was open: whether everything else public about the object and its company (where it flew, what its launch-mates and shell-mates did, whether it works today) separates a manoeuvre from a breakup at the first sets.
