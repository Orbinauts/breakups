## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Reports of breakups reach the truth through issues the owner accepts (`docs/decisions/0011`). `npm run truth:add` (`scripts/truth/add.ts`) checks one statement, as JSON or a filled breakup-report form, against the hand file's schema, refuses a machine-read source, an undated report, a report published before its event and a statement already held, appends it in the hand file's own layout and rebuilds the truth. The issue form `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/breakup-report.yml` files a report; the label `breakup-report-accepted`, applied with admin permission, makes `.github/workflows/breakup-report.yml` add it on a branch `report/issue-<number>` and run the maintenance workflow from it, which folds every waiting report in by its statements (`--missing-from`). The daily `.github/workflows/watch.yml` (`npm run maintenance:watch`, `scripts/maintenance/watch.ts`) reads NASA's Orbital Debris Quarterly News, ESA's Space Environment Report, Jonathan's Space Report and the feeds of EarthSky, Space.com, SpaceNews and Ars Technica, and opens an issue for each new notice, never a row: a prefilled `breakup-report` issue for a report, a `maintenance` issue to read and record a new quarterly news issue or ESA edition, judged new against `data/truth/odqn-coverage.json` and `data/truth/esa-ser.json` (issue #9).
- `serialiseStates` and `reviveStates` (`src/states.ts`, with the `DetectorStates` type): the states a run returns as text and back, for a caller that stores them between runs in a key-value store or a file. Plain `JSON.stringify` is not enough: it writes the states' maps as empty objects, and the infinities and NaN the detector keeps for "not yet", "never" and "not measured" as `null`. The text spells those numbers out as strings and each map as its entries, the encoding the scorecard's states files under `kpi/work/states` and the prospective release have always used, so those files still read and `scripts/kpi/states.ts` now writes and reads them with the library's encoding. A test runs the detector with its states passed through the text at every run and gets the same verdicts as an unbroken run (issue #27).
- Each published breakup carries `rowEvent` (`PublishedRowEvent`), the event as the ground-truth row it landed on dates it: `at`, `from`, `to`, `precision`, the `source` the row's date was resolved from and the row's id, or null where it landed on no row; `event` stays the detector's estimate. The id's day is now the row's where the row dates the event to the day or finer, else the estimate's, else the first raising's, and stays fixed once published. The files written before it are re-keyed once by the next run (`rekeyedBeforeRowEvent`): Luch (Olymp-K 1) becomes `40258-2026-01-30` and DMSP F-14 `24753-2024-12-19`. The files' version stays 1 (issue #11).
- The breakups fit to show, published as JSON on the repository's GitHub Pages (`docs/published.md`, `docs/decisions/0010`): `v1/breakups.json` (the list), `v1/breakups/<id>.json` (one breakup with its whole evidence, its landed ground-truth row and every verdict merged into it) and `v1/status.json` (the last run, the last day of sets read, the detector and the truth's sha256), each with a `version`. The package exports their types and `PUBLISHED_VERSION` (`src/published.ts`: `PublishedBreakupsFile`, `PublishedBreakupFile`, `PublishedStatusFile` and the types they name). The frozen detector's verdicts are landed on the truth on main as the scorecard lands them; a breakup is published confirmed when the detector confirmed it or an agency notice dated no earlier than the event names it, suspected when a news or analyst report names it, and nothing else is (`scripts/published/select.ts`, with an exported `overlaps` in `scripts/kpi/match.ts`). Each run merges into the list already published, keeping each breakup's id, a confirmed breakup for good and a suspected one until it expires or is dismissed (`scripts/published/merge.ts`), and writes the files (`scripts/published/files.ts`) through `npm run published`. Tests on hand-built fixtures and on the real 2025 and 2026 replays (`test/fixtures/published/`) check the exact lists. The files live on the orphan branch `published`, seeded once with the frozen tag's replay of 2025 (DMSP F-14, confirmed, no report); the daily prospective run publishes there after its replay and calls the Pages workflow, `docs.yml`, which now deploys the reference built from main and `v1/` from `published` together, on pushes to main and on that call, with the concurrency group on its deploy job alone. The README's live test names the files.
- A statement in the truth may carry `url`, the `https://` page it is read from (the article or post it is dated from, the first where its reference cites two). The four news and two analyst statements in `data/truth/hand.json` carry theirs; a statement with no link, such as the Space Force notice or a table's row, leaves it out, so it reads and serializes as before. `readStatement` refuses a `url` that is not an `https://` link. Links are never parsed out of `ref`.
- The early warning ranked, tested as a watch list (`docs/detector/early-warning-ranking.md`): `npm run kpi:rank` (`scripts/kpi/rank.ts`) builds one row per counted early warning from the year files, with its label, its raising and month, whether it was scoped and confirmed, and 36 features its verdict recorded at the raising, and measures the precision of the monthly top k (1, 3, 5, 10), the yearly top k (10, 20, 50) and a live list with a running threshold, with Wilson intervals, the headline targets each names before the first fragment, and the lead and timing of those. A hand-built percentile score and a small L2 logistic regression were fitted on the training years alone, and the logistic regression at the penalty 3 was frozen by a rule written before the held-out years were read (`FROZEN_RANKER`, 2026-10-11T17:34:50Z). Held out, the monthly top one is right 3.3% of the time (1.3% to 8.3%) and the top five 1.8%, against a base rate of 0.77% and the bar of 20% at five a month or fewer; a live list of about five a month names 6 of the 28 reported breakups before their first fragment at 2.1%. The ranked list is not useful by the bar, so the scorecard, the README and the live test do not carry it; the results page says so. Nothing the detector decides changed.

- The detector's seventh phase, `scoped-v6`, the scope study and the scoped line. The owner asked for a false-alarm rate under one in ten and for the way to nine in ten detected and one in ten false to be found by scoping. `scripts/kpi/scope.ts` reads a replay's year files and tests thirty scopes (dead payloads, rocket bodies, non-payloads, dead payloads with rocket bodies or every non-payload, low orbit, 600 to 2,000 kilometres, perigees above 500, objects older than a year or five, no persistent step in one or two years, a low drag term, every orbit but the highly elliptical, their combinations, and the exclusions the misses support), each a predicate on the parent at the time of the step applied alike to the reported events and the alerts, sweeps the strict gates the log allows on the even years (the step's size in sigma and in metres, the along-track impulse, the kick, the drag term's change, whether a manoeuvre explanation applied, and a persistence tier of up to seven days) and reads the odd years and the chronological halves at the point chosen, with Wilson intervals; `--frontier` prints every scope's training frontier with its validation reading and `--misses` the headline events by regime, class, perigee band, age, status at the event, record and cause. No scope reaches the target at any point; `src/scope.ts` (`inScope`, `passesGates`) and `DETECTOR_PARAMETERS.scope` implement the point nearest it by a pre-declared rule (parents launched five years or more before the step; a step of twenty sigma with a kick, the drag term changed two-fold; held seven days), every persistent step's row and verdict carry `scopeIn`, `scoped` and `ageFromLaunchDays`, and an alert raises one verdict of the new state `scoped` when its step reaches the tier. The scorecard carries a scoped table beside the headline line on every split (`scopedCellsOf`): the targets and alerts in the scope alike, both rates with their Wilson intervals, and the headline events the scope leaves out; the README carries it as its second table. The sixth decision record says why, and `docs/detector/d7-scope.md` records every hypothesis on both splits, the frontier, the always-missed analysis and the choice.

McDowell, CC-BY-4.0) into the Archive's `gcat/` and reads each payload's end of transmission, the date after which its owners could not command it, as the window GCAT's vague date spans (quarters and decades included); `npm run archive:annotate` carries it onto every year's body table as `endedFrom` and `endedOn`, beside the status; the side tarball carries the snapshot. - The detector's sixth phase, `fusion-v5`, manoeuvre discrimination as evidence. Five phases could not tell a breakup from a manoeuvre at the first sets from the element sets alone, and every alert on the held-out years was a false alarm, most of them lone manoeuvres on working satellites. This phase reads four more things of the object and weighs them with the record in one decision (`judgeStep` in `src/fusion.ts`), each a named parameter fitted on the even years and logged on every candidate so `npm run kpi:sweep` re-applies the whole decision offline. `src/maintenance.ts`: every object keeps the lowest and highest semi-major axis of each of its last twelve calendar months (frozen on a candidate at its opening), and a step whose post-step level lands within a fitted tolerance of a held range is a return to a held altitude, orbit maintenance. `src/siblings.ts`: a catalogue-wide ledger of the last four weeks' persistent steps, from which a step counts its launch-mates and shell-mates (the same inclination within a tenth of a degree, the semi-major axis within fifty kilometres) with a like step in the fortnight before, a coordinated campaign; and the median altitude of the launch's other payloads, towards which a young object's step is a deployment climb. The satellite catalogue's operational status (`OPS_STATUS_CODE`, carried as `opsStatus` on the element row and on `ArchivedBody`; `npm run archive:annotate` rewrites every year's body table from the latest snapshot without rebuilding the columns): a step on an object working today needs a breakup signature, a step on one nonoperational today with no record of moving itself owes no manoeuvre explanation; the status is today's, so the fitting note and the fifth decision record state the hindsight and the scorecard is reported with it on and off. The weighing: the record's own explanation is beaten by any breakup signature as before, but one explanation from outside the record outweighs the record's signatures (size, direction, geometry), and the nonoperational status is never outweighed. A lowering on a record of nothing but raises (a move against drag) and a floor on the geometry signature's radial component are parameters too, both off as fitted. With the status on, the validation false alarms fall from 15,546 to 4,764 (8,278 with it off) and the headline line stays at 11 detected, 2 late, 19 missed of 32; the false-alarm rate stays above 99%. The alert reads the object's one-step-ahead residual scatter in the second to fourth week after the step against its scatter before (the damage ratio), logged beside the drag term's ratio; `npm run kpi:sweep -- --separation` prints their quartiles on the detections against the false alarms (the damage ratio does not separate them). `scripts/kpi/causes.ts` reads two replays' year files side by side for the false alarms by cause and what each explanation removed. The fifth decision record says why the catalogue's metadata is read as evidence and how its hindsight is measured. The fitting note `docs/detector/d6-fitting.md` records every sweep, what each discriminator removed and what it cost, the status-off result beside the status-on one, and the false-alarm rate reached against the owner's target.

- The detector's fifth phase, `fusion-v4`, the Confirmed line made precise and the freeze. The third phase's confirmation by any plane-consistent piece under a quiet launch was wrong more often than right, and the wrong ones were clouds still being catalogued from a recent event (the parent a fragment itself, or the launch's cloud confirming the next step on any body of it) and single pieces under old launches. `src/run.ts` now judges every confirmation by five rules, each a parameter: the pieces must move at least the review list's metre a second from the parent; a debris-classed parent first catalogued under ninety days before its step is a fresh piece of the launch's own cloud and is confirmed by nothing; a launch remembers a cloud for a year (`src/fragments.ts`: a burst with consistent pieces, or a confirmation) and a later alert under it is confirmed only by pieces out of that cloud's plane and not where the parent itself lies in it, the plane distance taken both ways; a debris-classed parent under an active cloud is confirmed only by pieces nearer its plane than the cloud's nearest body; and a single piece confirms only a parent that is not debris under a launch that gained nothing in the year before and nothing else in the window. Every reading is kept on the alert (`src/fusion.ts`) and its log row, so `npm run kpi:sweep -- --confirmations` re-thresholds the rules offline from a replay with them off. `src/params.ts` carries `PARAMETER_PROVENANCE`, one entry per parameter with its kind (physical, policy or fitted) and the sweep or decision that chose it, which a test holds complete.
- The scorecard's confirmed block, a fourth table per split: of the targets the catalogue itself reported (new bodies under the launch within ninety days of the event), how many were alerted and confirmed and the days from the event and from the alert; of every alert confirmed, how many were on a reported event's step, how many on the right object with the step late, how many on nothing, with the precision both ways; and the confirmations before an agency's statement where the truth dates one (the notices alone). The KPI workflow recomputes the aggregate over the release's Archive and fails on main when it differs from the committed one. The README's "How well it does" table is read from the aggregate by the tests.
- `ElementSet`, the light row the detector reads, with `fromElements`, `apsidesOf` and `regimeOf`; and `runDetector`, the library's one entry point, with the `Verdict`, `Step`, `Notice` and `SpaceWeatherDay` shapes the scorecard scores. The detector itself is not written yet: this one carries the states through and raises nothing, so the scorecard shows zeros and says so.
- The Archive's tooling under `scripts/archive/`: the layout and columns of a year, the strip of a yearly bundle into them, the pull of the current year from the catalogue's history class (throttled, cached, ledgered), the satellite-catalogue and space-weather snapshots, and the upload and download of the years as release assets with a manifest of hashes.
