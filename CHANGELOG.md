- The detector's seventh phase, `scoped-v6`, the scope study and the scoped line. No scope reaches the target at any point; `src/scope.ts` (`inScope`, `passesGates`) and `DETECTOR_PARAMETERS.scope` implement the point nearest it by a pre-declared rule (parents launched five years or more before the step; a step of twenty sigma with a kick, the drag term changed two-fold; held seven days), every persistent step's row and verdict carry `scopeIn`, `scoped` and `ageFromLaunchDays`, and an alert raises one verdict of the new state `scoped` when its step reaches the tier. The scorecard carries a scoped table beside the headline line on every split (`scopedCellsOf`): the targets and alerts in the scope alike, both rates with their Wilson intervals, and the headline events the scope leaves out; the README carries it as its second table. - The detector's sixth phase, `fusion-v5`, manoeuvre discrimination as evidence. Five phases could not tell a breakup from a manoeuvre at the first sets from the element sets alone, and every alert on the held-out years was a false alarm, most of them lone manoeuvres on working satellites. This phase reads four more things of the object and weighs them with the record in one decision (`judgeStep` in `src/fusion.ts`), each a named parameter fitted on the even years and logged on every candidate so `npm run kpi:sweep` re-applies the whole decision offline. `src/maintenance.ts`: every object keeps the lowest and highest semi-major axis of each of its last twelve calendar months (frozen on a candidate at its opening), and a step whose post-step level lands within a fitted tolerance of a held range is a return to a held altitude, orbit maintenance. `src/siblings.ts`: a catalogue-wide ledger of the last four weeks' persistent steps, from which a step counts its launch-mates and shell-mates (the same inclination within a tenth of a degree, the semi-major axis within fifty kilometres) with a like step in the fortnight before, a coordinated campaign; and the median altitude of the launch's other payloads, towards which a young object's step is a deployment climb. The satellite catalogue's operational status (`OPS_STATUS_CODE`, carried as `opsStatus` on the element row and on `ArchivedBody`; `npm run archive:annotate` rewrites every year's body table from the latest snapshot without rebuilding the columns): a step on an object working today needs a breakup signature, a step on one nonoperational today with no record of moving itself owes no manoeuvre explanation; the status is today's, so the fitting note and the fifth decision record state the hindsight and the scorecard is reported with it on and off. The weighing: the record's own explanation is beaten by any breakup signature as before, but one explanation from outside the record outweighs the record's signatures (size, direction, geometry), and the nonoperational status is never outweighed. A lowering on a record of nothing but raises (a move against drag) and a floor on the geometry signature's radial component are parameters too, both off as fitted. With the status on, the validation false alarms fall from 15,546 to 4,764 (8,278 with it off) and the headline line stays at 11 detected, 2 late, 19 missed of 32; the false-alarm rate stays above 99%. The alert reads the object's one-step-ahead residual scatter in the second to fourth week after the step against its scatter before (the damage ratio), logged beside the drag term's ratio; `npm run kpi:sweep -- --separation` prints their quartiles on the detections against the false alarms (the damage ratio does not separate them). `scripts/kpi/causes.ts` reads two replays' year files side by side for the false alarms by cause and what each explanation removed. The fifth decision record says why the catalogue's metadata is read as evidence and how its hindsight is measured. The fitting note `docs/detector/d6-fitting.md` records every sweep, what each discriminator removed and what it cost, the status-off result beside the status-on one, and the false-alarm rate reached against the owner's target.

- The detector's fifth phase, `fusion-v4`, the Confirmed line made precise and the freeze. The third phase's confirmation by any plane-consistent piece under a quiet launch was wrong more often than right, and the wrong ones were clouds still being catalogued from a recent event (the parent a fragment itself, or the launch's cloud confirming the next step on any body of it) and single pieces under old launches. `src/run.ts` now judges every confirmation by five rules, each a parameter: the pieces must move at least the review list's metre a second from the parent; a debris-classed parent first catalogued under ninety days before its step is a fresh piece of the launch's own cloud and is confirmed by nothing; a launch remembers a cloud for a year (`src/fragments.ts`: a burst with consistent pieces, or a confirmation) and a later alert under it is confirmed only by pieces out of that cloud's plane and not where the parent itself lies in it, the plane distance taken both ways; a debris-classed parent under an active cloud is confirmed only by pieces nearer its plane than the cloud's nearest body; and a single piece confirms only a parent that is not debris under a launch that gained nothing in the year before and nothing else in the window. Every reading is kept on the alert (`src/fusion.ts`) and its log row, so `npm run kpi:sweep -- --confirmations` re-thresholds the rules offline from a replay with them off. `src/params.ts` carries `PARAMETER_PROVENANCE`, one entry per parameter with its kind (physical, policy or fitted) and the sweep or decision that chose it, which a test holds complete.
- The scorecard's confirmed block, a fourth table per split: of the targets the catalogue itself reported (new bodies under the launch within ninety days of the event), how many were alerted and confirmed and the days from the event and from the alert; of every alert confirmed, how many were on a reported event's step, how many on the right object with the step late, how many on nothing, with the precision both ways; and the confirmations before an agency's statement where the truth dates one (the notices alone). The matcher records on each event the pieces within ninety days and the parent's sets between the event and the catalogue, and on a false alarm the reported event on the same parent whose window ended within the month before the step.
- The per-event dissection, `docs/detector/events.md`, written by `scripts/kpi/events.ts` with the aggregate: one row per scoreable event over every year with the outcome, the cause read off the detector's own log for the parent (raised and by what; late with no post set before the catalogue or persistence after it; missed by the record gate, a ramping fit, a step taken back, a fade, a lapse, the margin, no sets before the catalogue, or a parent that did not move; undetectable), the lead over the catalogue, the confirmation and the timing error, with the totals by cause.
- `docs/detector/README.md`, the design as built: one paragraph per module, the gates, the states, what each phase bought in the scorecard's numbers, and the ceiling analysis with its evidence; and `docs/detector/d5-fitting.md`, the confirmation rules swept on the training split with every confirmation listed before and after on both splits.
- `npm run kpi -- --param name=value --out <dir>` replays with a parameter overridden into another directory, so a trial never overwrites the committed results; `npm run kpi -- --rematch` scores the year files present again from their verdicts, without a replay.

### Fixed

- The KPI workflow could not make the committed aggregate again from the ninth phase on: the scorecard read the catalogue over every built year of the Archive (the Confirmed line's targets, and from the eleventh phase the reentries the detector is given), and the workflow holds a year and the one before it, so each year was scored against another catalogue than beside every other year. The scorecard reads it from the catalogue index, `<archive>/index/catalogue-index.json.gz`, built from the whole Archive by `npm run kpi:index` (`scripts/kpi/catalogue-index.ts`) and shipped with the release's side files: one row per body, and each truth row's parent's last set before its window, with each year's build time recorded. The scorecard refuses to run without it, with a stale one, or with one built for a truth lacking a row's parent. `npm run archive:upload -- --side-only` refreshes the side files and the release's manifest without the years, and the download checks that the index describes the released years.
- The log kept the refusal bits of an attribution row to four significant digits, so a refusal of 16,384 or more was written as other bits (16,384 as 16,380): the per-event dissection named some misses' causes wrongly. The bits are kept whole.
- The detector's catalogue mark took every designated number, and a body designated under a launch but numbered in the analyst ranges lifted it in 2020 above every piece catalogued after: from mid-2020 no new body was above the mark and no alert could be confirmed by pieces. The mark reads numbers below the analyst floor, as the scorecard's always did.
- The scorecard's catalogue mark followed a body's earliest set epoch, so a yearly bundle's old debris catalogued in August with set epochs from January sat above a breakup's real pieces and put the catalogue's time months late (Cosmos 2392's ullage motor in 2005: ninety-seven days instead of thirty-two); and a fragment's first set was dated by its epoch, which a newly catalogued piece back-fills from before it appeared. Both now follow the catalogue's publication order by the replay's clock, as the detector reads it. On the validation split the headline line reads one detection fewer (Nadezhda 2's rocket body's alert came after its pieces, which the old mark had hidden) and the catalogue's time for Cosmos 2251 and Iridium 33 moves from 3 March to 17 February 2009; the earlier phases' leads over the catalogue were overstated by the same fault. The fourth decision record says so.

- The detector's third phase, `fusion-v3`, the fragments as confirmation and the event's time. `src/fragments.ts` keeps a record per launch (and a pseudo-launch for each analyst range) of every body seen with its first set's orbit, and the catalogue-wide high-water mark of designated numbers against time; on a run that brings a launch new bodies it reads a burst over the launch's own background (a Poisson tail of the fortnight's count against the year before, floored), the numbering (a body at or under the mark at the event, or with a set before it, is a returning old body), the plane of each piece against each candidate parent with the node adjusted for each orbit's own J2 precession, and the cloud's velocity and Gabbard spread, with deployments and a young launch's own objects excluded. `src/fusion.ts` and `src/run.ts` open an alert on every persistent step, suspected or manoeuvre, that stays thirty days: pieces new by numbering, in the parent's plane and first published after the step confirm it (`confirmed`), as does a public notice naming the object; nothing in thirty days expires it (`expired`); bodies that came and were every one old dismiss it (`dismissed`); and a burst with consistent pieces under a launch with no step on any parent is listed as `cloud-only` for review, never a detection. A manoeuvre the pieces confirm is promoted and says so. While open, the alert keeps reading the object's sets against the frozen pre-step model and logs whether the step still held after three and five post sets and after one, three, five and seven days, and the tracking cadence after the step. `src/event-time.ts` times a suspected step from the two sets either side of it: both propagated with the engine on a minute grid from six hours before the pre set's epoch to the post set's, the along-track separation fitted as a line whose root is the event and whose scatter over the drift is its sigma, and the per-revolution closest approaches listed so the revolution ambiguity is stated; the suspected verdict carries it as its `event`, and the scorecard's timing error on the validation detections falls from a median of eighteen hours to thirty-five minutes.
- The scorecard's third table, the precision per persistence tier, read off the alert rows without a replay: what raising only once the step had held for that many post sets or days would have left of the false alarms and of the detections that still beat the catalogue; and the confirmed lines of the alerts table: how many alerts the fragments or a notice confirmed, how many of those had matched a reported event, how many nothing, and the median days from the alert. A confirmed verdict on a step already alerted lands as that alert's confirmation, not as a second alert. - The detector's second phase, `step-v2`, the object's own record and the impulse a step implies: `src/impulse.ts` reads a measured step by Gauss's variational equations as a tangential component (from the step in the semi-major axis, at the point on the orbit the two apsides place it), a normal component (from the inclination step, where it is at least a hundredth of a degree) and the least radial component the eccentricity change needs beyond a single tangential impulse's reach, with that excess as a chi-squared; `src/manoeuvre.ts` keeps on every object its accepted steps over the last two years (whatever verdict each got, in a bounded list in the object's state) and classes a new step by them alone as station-keeping, campaign, disposal, occasional or unique, with the step's size over the median past step, its direction against theirs, and the radial kick as the tests by which a step on a manoeuvring object is still raised; the catalogue's class of the object is read in one place only, where a young payload's first raise starts a campaign, never as a gate. `src/run.ts` raises a persistent step as `suspected` only where the record does not explain it, and closes the rest with the terminal verdict `manoeuvre`, which the scorecard counts as nothing. Every closed candidate's log row carries the impulse and the history, so `npm run kpi:sweep` re-thresholds the manoeuvre gate offline.
- `docs/detector/d2-fitting.md`: what the manoeuvre gate's sweeps on the training split showed, what was chosen and what it cost.
- The detector's first phase, `step-v1`, the parent-step channel on every object in every orbit: `src/series.ts` keeps a ring of each object's last thirty-two sets with the one-step-ahead residual each left, de-duplicates re-published epochs, slots older epochs in, rejects unreadable sets and holds a set too far from the orbit (fifty kilometres of semi-major axis, half a degree of inclination, five degrees of node off its J2 precession) until the next set confirms it, and measures the object's own scatter (a robust short scatter floored by a half-year memory, a regime prior scaled by the drag term and the day's flux and Ap until there are residuals); `src/model.ts` fits a Huber-weighted local polynomial per regime and predicts with the extrapolation's leverage and a drag term that grows with B* and the gap; `src/environment.ts` reads each run's residuals by band (fifty kilometres of perigee height in low orbit) for the day's common-mode drag and live scale, and the space-weather indices for the storm hold and the prior; `src/step.ts` estimates the step over the post sets with a free settling slope, the later sets' ratio to the first jump, the evidence for a step against a drift drag could produce, and a two-sided CUSUM; `src/run.ts` runs the three passes and raises `watch` on a quiet object's first post set, `suspected` when the step persists across two post sets over eighteen hours (six above low orbit) or three, with three under a storm hold or run stress, and `dismissed` when the next sets undo it; `src/params.ts` holds every threshold, each tagged physical, policy or fitted, with its provenance; `src/log.ts` writes one row per candidate with every signal and the step's trajectory, for the offline sweeps `npm run kpi:sweep` runs over a year's rows.

- The target is on Confirmed alerts. On the owner's decision of 2026-10-10 (the seventh decision record, `docs/decisions/0007-the-target-is-on-confirmed-alerts.md`) the nine-in-ten and one-in-ten targets move from the alert on the parent's step to the Confirmed line: a breakup the detector attributes to a parent from fresh fragments in its plane, with or without a step seen first. The scorecard's headline is the Confirmed line (`confirmedCellsOf`, `firstConfirmationsOf` in `scripts/kpi/kpi.ts`): the targets are the scoreable events whose launch gained at least one fresh piece in the parent's plane within 180 days (`fragmentsOf` in the new `scripts/kpi/catalogue.ts`, the Archive read once over every year), events with none listed apart per line; a detection is the first confirmed verdict by pieces on the parent within 180 days after the event, across the years; a confirmed verdict matching no reported event is a false alarm (`landConfirmed` in `scripts/kpi/match.ts`); both rates with Wilson intervals on every split; the lead over the first agency statement, the lag after the first fresh piece's publication and the timing error. It is the README's first table; the parent-step tables follow as secondary measurements, the headline line labelled as such. The per-event dissection gives each event's Confirmed outcome and its cause. `DETECTOR_NAME` is `confirmed-v7`. The fourth decision record carries a status note pointing to the seventh.
- The scorecard matches a step to a reported event with a margin per regime: a day in low and medium orbits, five days in highly elliptical and geosynchronous ones, where sets are days apart and the stated instant is itself inferred. The fourth decision record says why.
- The scorecard replays the years in order and carries the states from each year into the next, since the detector's verdict now depends on the object's record over two years; `npm run kpi -- --all` does so and leaves each year's end states under `kpi/work/states/`, from which `npm run kpi:year -- <year>` replays a year alone to the same result. The KPI workflow replays the years in one sequential job.
- `kpi/years/` is no longer committed: the per-year files are artifacts of the KPI workflow and are made again by `npm run kpi -- --all`; only `kpi/latest.json` and `kpi/latest.md` are committed.
- The scorecard writes and reads a year's file gzipped (`kpi/years/<year>.json.gz`): with every persistent step raised before the manoeuvre classing, a year holds tens of thousands of verdicts and log rows, tens of megabytes plain and a few compressed. The KPI workflow uploads the compressed file.
- The ground truth: every on-orbit breakup and anomalous event an authority reported, 1961 to today, as `data/breakups.json`, built by `npm run truth` from NASA's History of On-Orbit Satellite Fragmentations (16th edition, read from its text), GCAT's Space Debris Clouds table, and hand-entered statements from NASA's Orbital Debris Quarterly News, a U.S. Space Forces-Space notice, ESA's Space Environment Report, named analysts, news reports and the public catalogue; each row keeps every source's statement verbatim beside the values resolved from them and lists where the sources disagree. The report `docs/truth.md` says what was built from what.
- `readBreakups`, `readStatements` and the types of the truth, exported for whoever reads the file.
- The scorecard under `scripts/kpi/`: `npm run kpi:year -- <year>` replays a year of the Archive through the detector in runs of four hours, in the catalogue's publication order, warmed up from the previous year, and matches what it raised against the ground truth by the rule of the fourth decision record (a detection is an alert on the parent's own step before the first fragment's set and before any notice; late, duplicate, exempt and false alarms apart; parents with no public elements out of the denominator; breakups of confidence A and B on the headline line, coarser confidences and anomalous events on their own); `npm run kpi -- --aggregate` folds the years into `kpi/latest.json` and `kpi/latest.md`, the cells per split (the odd years held out, the even, every year, the last three, and before and from 2018). The KPI workflow recomputes the aggregate over the release's Archive and fails on main when it differs from the committed one. The README's "How well it does" table is read from the aggregate by the tests.
- `ElementSet`, the light row the detector reads, with `fromElements`, `apsidesOf` and `regimeOf`; and `runDetector`, the library's one entry point, with the `Verdict`, `Step`, `Notice` and `SpaceWeatherDay` shapes the scorecard scores. The detector itself is not written yet: this one carries the states through and raises nothing, so the scorecard shows zeros and says so.
- The Archive's tooling under `scripts/archive/`: the layout and columns of a year, the strip of a yearly bundle into them, the pull of the current year from the catalogue's history class (throttled, cached, ledgered), the satellite-catalogue and space-weather snapshots, and the upload and download of the years as release assets with a manifest of hashes.
