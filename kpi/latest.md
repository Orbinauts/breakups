# The scorecard, as last run

Generated by `npm run kpi -- --aggregate` over the years 2005 to 2026 of the Archive with the confirmed-v10 detector of `@orbinauts/breakups` 0.1.0 on `@orbinauts/engine` 0.1.2, `satellite.js` 7.1.0 and `astronomy-engine` 2.1.19, against the ground truth whose SHA-256 is `143a5213888f7afae77eb5883bc8047cdad85385dd2a4cf8099d10bead9ccf03`. The results page, `docs/results.md`, carries the Confirmed headline and the validation split's tables; the tests check them against `kpi/latest.json`. Never edited by hand. The per-event dissection is in `docs/detector/events.md`, written with this file.

**The Confirmed line is the target** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-10, `docs/decisions/0007-the-target-is-on-confirmed-alerts.md`). A detection is a confirmed verdict by pieces on a reported event's parent, raised within 180 days after the event's window, with or without a step seen first on the parent: the confirmation of an alert on the parent's step, or the attribution of fresh pieces in the parent's plane to it. The targets are the scoreable events whose launch gained at least one fresh piece in the parent's plane within 180 days in the Archive (a body under the launch numbered above the catalogue's mark at the event, classed as a fragment is, first published after it, its plane within the scorecard's own bound of the parent's last set before the event: the detector's plane test with its tolerances as they stood on 2026-10-12, or its orbit normal within a degree of the parent's, which holds at near-zero inclination where the node is undefined); events with no such piece, or no orbit of the parent before the event to test a plane against, are listed apart and out of the denominator. An event naming a launch and no catalogued parent (a burst under the launch, a separation of an object the public catalogue does not list) is scored by its launch: a confirmed verdict on any body of the launch within its window and the 180 days after it detects it, and it is a target where the launch gained fresh pieces in the window, with no parent's plane to test them against. A window wider than 93 days, or with no lower edge, keeps its row out of every line. A confirmation is assigned to the parent's reported event nearest the detector's instant of the event (else its raising), so a later event's confirmation never counts for an earlier one. The first confirmation of an event counts, across the years; later ones are duplicates; a confirmed verdict matching no reported event is unreported: pending while it was raised under 90 days before the truth's date (2026-10-07, the latest day the truth knows of), in neither count, and a false alarm after that. A report naming no catalogued object exempts a confirmation inside its window only where it names the verdict's launch or its object's regime; one naming neither counts it in a cell of its own, neither exempt nor false. The false-alarm rate is over the detections on every line, each rate with its Wilson 95% interval. A confirmation by a notice alone is not the Confirmed line's, and a confirmed verdict under a crewed station's launch is exempt: what a station sheds is released on purpose; so is one under the launch of a crewed spacecraft or a station's cargo craft, unless it names the launch's rocket body. Every split scores from 2006: 2005, the Archive's first year, is the detector's warm-up, replayed and scored on no split. A verdict naming a launch and its candidate bodies (twins the pieces cannot tell apart) is matched on the body it names first in the headline rows; the rows marked as the twins matched at the launch read it as a detection where a reported event's parent is among its candidates. The lead is read two ways, positive where the confirmation came first: the days from the confirmation to the first agency notice dated to the day (a U.S. Space Forces-Space notice, to the day it was seen; ESA's report; any agency's statement dated to the day), and to NASA's first quarterly news issue, dated to the first day of its month, the earliest it can have been public; the lag is the days from the first fresh piece's publication to the confirmation; the timing error is the detector's instant of the event against the truth's, where both state one.

The early warning's lines follow each split's Confirmed tables, as the scorecard measured them before the target moved to the Confirmed line. A target there is a scoreable reported event whose parent had at least five public element sets in the sixty days before it; an event with fewer is undetectable and out of the denominator. Detected: an alert on the parent's own step before the first fragment's set was published and before any notice. Late: the same alert after that. The false-alarm rate is false alarms over false alarms plus detections on every line, with its Wilson 95% interval, as the detection rate has. The scoped table is the headline line restricted to the detector's scope (`DETECTOR_PARAMETERS.scope`, `docs/detector/d7-scope.md`); both its rates carry their Wilson 95% intervals, and the headline events the scope leaves out are counted beside them. A year with no publication times (one stripped from a yearly bundle) times the catalogue by the first fragment's epoch, a lower bound. The tier table reads every alert's later sets: what raising only once the step had held for that many post sets or days would have left of the false alarms and of the detections that still beat the catalogue. The last table is the step alerts confirmed: of the targets the catalogue itself reported (new bodies under the launch within ninety days of the event), how many alerts on the parent's step the fragments or a notice confirmed and how long after; and of every step alert confirmed, how many were on a reported event's step, how many on the right object with the step late, and how many on nothing.
